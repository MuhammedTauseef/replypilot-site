Measuring whether it's working

An AI store assistant should be measured like a salesperson. Track conversation-to-order rate: what share of chats end in a purchase? Track average order value for assisted vs. unassisted sessions — good recommendations usually lift it. And track the questions the assistant couldn't answer; every gap is a product page or policy that needs clarifying, which improves the store for everyone. Review transcripts weekly at first. You'll quickly see patterns: the same three questions, the same two products compared. Feed those insights back into your merchandising, and the assistant becomes a source of business intelligence, not just a sales tool. The first conversation worth building

If all of this feels like a lot, start with exactly one conversation: the sizing question. Pick the product category where sizing causes the most returns \u2014 shoes, clothing, anything fitted. Teach the assistant to ask three questions: what size do you usually wear, do you prefer a snug or relaxed fit, and what will you use it for. From those answers it recommends one size, explains why, and links straight to checkout. That single flow can cut returns noticeably, because most returns come from guessing wrong, not from disliking the product. Once it works, copy the pattern: the same three-question structure handles gift recommendations, compatibility checks, and bundle suggestions. One good conversation becomes the template for ten. You do not need a grand AI strategy. You need one helpful chat that pays for itself, then another.

How to Set It Up When You're Not a Tech Person

You do not need to know code. Setting up a store assistant is mostly typing what you already know: your product names, your prices, your shipping times, your return rules. If you can write a good product description, you can train a store assistant.

Start with three steps. First, connect your product list — most stores can import from Shopify, WooCommerce, or a simple spreadsheet. Second, write your policies in plain words: shipping times, returns, delivery areas. Third, list the ten questions customers ask you most, and write the answers the way you would say them in a text message.

You will spot the same unanswered question popping up again and again — that is your next product page to fix or your next answer to teach. Stores that do this weekly review end up with an assistant that feels sharper every month. Stores that skip it end up with an assistant frozen in time, answering last season's questions.

Will the assistant know my actual product catalogue?

Yes — it learns your real products, prices, and stock levels, not a generic script. When something sells out, it stops recommending it. Shoppers get accurate answers instead of dead ends.

Can the store assistant recover abandoned carts?

Yes. When a shopper leaves with items in the cart, it can follow up with a helpful nudge — answering the question that stopped them, or reminding them the item is still available. Cart recovery is pure found revenue.

Where should I place the AI assistant on my store?

Start with product pages and the cart — that is where purchase decisions happen. A floating chat button on every page works too. Measure which placement drives more conversations, then expand.
